    CROBA FIELD GUIDE

    A small step into
    the unknown.

    Salvage and building. Pick up small crashed-cargo debris with E / Interact. Open CRAFT or press K: three debris and two frozen basin leaves make a Broken Hammer. Keep the hammer and use six debris to craft a Workbench. Equip its kit in one of your five quick slots and press F / USE on dry land to place it. Stand within four meters to craft a Rusty Copper Knife or Rock Chipper. The knife cuts edible leaves; the chipper removes loose seabed rocks for debris. Recover the Identifier in the truck wreck cabin to identify debris, and the Builder Tool inside the original basin cargo crate. The builder uses eight debris for a reinforced 12 × 16 meter Universal Base. Its fitted poles and automatic stairs adapt to clear land; white walls with smooth gray stripes, rounded dark blue edge trim and a flat gray hexagon floor with dark blue patterns sit beneath a 4.5 meter ceiling. The roof is mostly blue with white accents. Four dimmed sky lights illuminate the interior. Open BUILD or press V to choose a base, workbench, chair, table, bunk, cabinet or planter. Furniture fits inside a Universal Base. Select a blueprint, rotate with R or ROTATE, then F / USE places it. The preview turns red when obstructed; materials are spent only on successful placement. Q / RECLAIM removes nearby furniture and recovers its debris when your pack has room. Equipment, furniture and construction stay in your expedition save.

    Explore your shattered ship, the glacier underside, and the sandy seabed. Record each discovery with E. You can keep exploring after your field log is complete. Blue hollow tube coral grows on the Arctic seabed. Glowing purple plants hang beneath glaciers, and every ice island has a spiky underside. Blue seaweed, or cobalt ribbon kelp, sways above sediment channels and rocky underwater terraces. Dive into the northern Blue Kelp Forest or the southern Orange Grass Field beneath the Arctic open water. Four-eyed, horned Frost Hounds hunt among the orange grass. Black Void Fish patrol in packs near the deepest blue-ocean floor. Purple lantern jellyfish drift throughout the blue ocean. Keep clear of the giant lanterns’ pale pink trailing stingers.

    Cliff Biome. The original central islands share the same bare snow and blue ice as the other islands outside the Icy Basin. Their old water gaps remain open. Dive below to enter deep branching cracks in the sandy seabed. Icy Basin now extends far north beyond Croba’s former border. The northern basin lies across the water beyond the iceberg northeast of the wreck. Explore the massive new plateau, snowy peaks, and valleys. Beaked, four-eyed Tundra Hogwongs graze on the land. Look for its white map highlight, snow-covered ground and frozen white plants. Natural ice walls form winding passages through the basin; the ice centipede stays outside. Open Backpack (I) to eat rations, leaves and Crobon Bird eggs, or assign items to five quick slots. Keys 1–5 select a slot; F / USE activates it. Find the COPPERSTUDIO trunk on the original Icy Basin island: open it and collect its solid copper yo-yo. A Rain and Copper concept-art T-shirt, copper wire and ingots remain as decorations. Hold F, the left mouse button, or touch USE to keep the yo-yo extended; release to reel it back. Groups of one to three peaceful, one-eyed Woodlons draw nutrients from every bone pile and bone nest in the Basins. Warmth-beacon-style wall lamps mark old cave visits. A huge crashed cargo hauler lies on the large protected glacier east of your original crash site. Its truck-like cab leads a rear flatbed with shipping boxes still strapped in place. E rips edible leaves or steals a nearby nest egg, provoking its parents. Older island colonies have two nests; Icy Basin colonies have four. Swim into openings in the Basin ice to find breathable caverns. All four mainland passages connect through a central chamber; hold Dive to leave through a low entrance. Crobon Birds take turns nesting, lay replacement eggs after 45 seconds of sitting, and hatch eggs after 60 seconds of brooding. White furry chicks stay on their own island or ship wing and grow into blue-gray adults over 150 seconds of nearby play. Hunger drains during exploration; eating restores it. Croba saves your location, vitals, backpack, discoveries, harvested plants, eggs, incubation and chick growth in named browser slots, with export/import backups. Croba is one prepared world. Every reload, new game, and restart returns you to the same map and wildlife starting positions. Learn its routes; the animals still move and react as you explore.

    Follow the ice and ocean coast southeast from the wreck to Violet Spruce Forest, Croba’s woodland biome. Purple spruce, ferns, lavender flowers, and grasses grow above a mossy orange shoreline. Walk around solid trunks, rocks, and fallen logs along the trail to the ocean overlook. Forest soil fades into sandy beaches, darker wet sand, and shallow rocky shelves. Softer foam follows the waterline between the rocky headlands. Enter by the low beach at its northwestern tip; swim up the gentle shore. The expanded world is twice as wide and twice as tall. The forest reaches the eastern and southern stone borders, with clear weather, purple grass, and woodland hills. The low beach still leads onto the inland trail. Your field log still has three discoveries. The bestiary records fifteen creature species. Six bark-skinned Tree Stalkers hide among the spruce. Each creeps toward you after spotting you, then sprints when it closes within 10 m. Break its sight behind terrain or reach water to escape. If it catches you, its claws lift you toward a mosquito-like feeding tube; your view fades to black before your rescue tether returns you to the wreck. With sound on, soft wind and footsteps accompany forest walks. Rare ambient sounds drift through the forest and Basin Caves, with several quiet minutes between them.

    Red Root Crabs live underwater across the kelp forest, orange grass fields and blue-ocean floor, carrying golden spiral shells with small marine plants growing from their ridges. Gold Rock Bugs climb seabed rocks and ice, curling into crevices with flexing, gold-edged red armor, fourteen tucked legs, two mouth tentacles and one large embedded gold eye that follows nearby explorers.

    Juvenile Artic Crobonic Squid. Eight peaceful juveniles live across the Arctic, kelp, grass fields and blue ocean. They are 3 meters long, with the adults’ oval, flat-sided blue heads and segmented shell armor. Four large yellow eyes have slim black outlines, horizontal squid pupils and folded blinking lids, without brow ridges. Blue cheek mandibles flank a dark beak above a peach plated belly. Their smooth head sides have no ear-like fins. Eight dark gray tentacles have blue bands. Their pupils follow swimmers within 10 m. They cruise gently in all directions, turning, pitching, and banking their whole bodies. Their tentacles pulse with the water jets, opening during refill and gathering during thrust. Swimmers can push them; look for their blue circles on the map.

    Adult Artic Crobonic Squids. Three 30 m hunters patrol the blue ocean. Their oval, flat-sided blue heads measure 8 m across, with four small yellow squid eyes and folded blinking lids, armored blue sides, a central blue stripe reaching the rear siphons, and skin-colored bellies. Short, sharp blue mandibles curve inward from the cheeks around the exposed beak. Neither adults nor juveniles have ear-like side fins. Each of their eight tentacles ends in a hooked pincer. Their swimming strokes become faster and stronger when they spot prey. They can grab and eat other creatures, which return after a short delay. Keep clear when they notice you. When adults or juveniles swim upward, their free tentacles gather beneath the body with each jet pulse and fan gently outward between strokes.

    Ice Centipede. One hunts across the ice islands: 60 meters long, with a white underside and a gray, spiked back. It crosses gaps and climbs island edges, so island hopping may not break its chase. One bite is fatal: its jaws close in a brief eating sequence before the rescue tether returns you to the wreck. Find its red map marker and reach the water before it strikes.

    Swim east to the dark blue ocean, 100 meters deep. Sediment channels and stepped coastal rock lead into the abyss, where submerged outcrops rise above the deep basin. Your suit automatically enters deep-dive mode here: hold C or Shift to descend at 12 m/s and Space to ascend at the same speed. A full oxygen tank lasts 500 seconds in the blue ocean and 100 seconds in the Arctic. Swim up to clear steep underwater rock faces. With sound on, Cold Air plays first in the Arctic, followed by The Bound Arctic. The two songs alternate and fade away as you leave the ice. Inside the Basin Caves, Cold World takes over and loops until you leave. A permanent snowstorm blows above the Arctic. The blue ocean has no snowstorm, with thin ice fragments drifting on its surface. Icebergs border the Arctic, while orange stone cliffs surround the ocean. A yellow-marked arch in Shell Field’s northern rock border opens into Sulfuric Land. Follow its shallow ramp to warm mineral hills, basalt ridges, geyser ponds and steaming fumaroles. Four-eyed Sulfur Maw Lizards bask nearby. The biome extends five times its original area, with three adult Crobonic Artic Sulfur Worms, each 100 metres long and 9 metres wide, leaping through its open mineral flats. Their four mouth shields open as they breach. Jumping on this land alerts the worms. Listen for approaching rumbles and dodge sideways when the ground swells. A fatal strike draws you into its mouth before the four shields close and the rescue tether returns you. Return through the same arch to reach the ocean. A second salt-crusted opening deeper in Shell Field’s northern cliff enters the flooded Salt Cave. Two looping side halls and a smaller gallery have scorched stone, sulfur-stained salt crystals, murky yellow water and sparse withered plants. Bubbling hot sulfur seeps burn your suit nearby; swim higher or go around them. Newly hatched sulfur worms burrow into the cave and swim through its halls with flexible bodies and four opening mouth shields. They grow here before leaving through the geyser shaft. The cave has no separate starting worm population. Barnacle colonies cling to the walls and floor, filtering the water with small moving feelers. Related colonies grow on submerged seabed shelves throughout the ocean, kelp forest and grass fields. Barnacles remain underwater, away from dry land and the tops of the ice islands. Swim up the shaft to emerge through the salt-ringed geyser pond, or return through the cliff. Pale pink lines on the sector map show the halls and both entrances.

    Growing wildlife. Crobon Birds, Woodlons, Hogwongs, Red Root Crabs, Gold Rock Bugs, sulfur worms, Sulfur Maw Lizards, Red Fish, Frost Hounds and Void Fish reproduce. Woodlons give birth to soft white babies that follow their parents and grow into adult coats; they do not lay eggs. Baby sulfur worms grow in the Salt Cave before leaving through the geyser shaft. Aging adults slow down, and mature local offspring replace retiring elders. Breeding, growth and aging progress stay in your expedition save.
